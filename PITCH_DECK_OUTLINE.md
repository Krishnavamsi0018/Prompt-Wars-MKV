# Pitch Deck Outline — fill in fast at 4:15

Aim for ~5–6 min. Lead with the prompt strategy — that's what's being judged most heavily, so don't bury it at the end.

## 1. Title
Project name + one-line tagline.

## 2. The Problem
What the brief asked for, in your own words. 2 sentences max.

## 3. Our Angle
The specific, slightly non-obvious approach you took — this is your Creativity moment. Say explicitly what the "obvious" solution would've been and why you didn't build that.

## 4. Prompt Strategy (the core of the pitch)
Walk through it like a story, not a spec dump:
- "Our first attempt at the prompt did X, but it failed because Y."
- "So we restructured it to enforce Z."
- Show the before/after if you have a slide for it.
This directly answers what judges are told to weight at 30%.

## 5. How It Works
One clean diagram: input → prompt → model → validation → output. Be ready to explain each arrow (WHAT/HOW/WHY).

## 6. Live Demo
Primary: live link. Backup: local version. Fallback: recorded video/screenshots. Know which one you're using before you're called up.

## 7. Accuracy & Edge Cases
Briefly show you tested it against weird/invalid input, not just the happy path.

## 8. Relevance & Impact
Tie every major feature back to a specific line in the brief. Don't let this feel generic.

## 9. What We'd Do Next
One honest limitation + how you'd extend it. Shows self-awareness, costs you nothing.

## Delivery notes
- Whoever explains the prompt strategy should be the one who actually iterated on it — don't let it sound rehearsed-but-hollow.
- Rehearse the WHAT/HOW/WHY for your top 2 features out loud at least once before 5 PM.
